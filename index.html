02 / MY VISUAL READ

Black is the stage.
Pink is the signal.

On YG’s official profile [1], I noticed an oversized all-caps group name, a wide portrait of all four members, generous white space, and a mostly black, white and grey page with a small coral accent. I’m carrying the high contrast and scale into my work and translating that accent into vivid pink. That palette is my interpretation—not an official colour specification.

INK / SIGNAL / GLOW / SPACE

03 / THE DESIGN DECISIONS

Bold at first glance.
Thoughtful up close.

  • COLOURBlack, hot pink, soft pink and warm white.
  • TYPEOne heavy headline balanced with clear, readable supporting text.
  • LAYOUTA single clear focal point, plenty of breathing room and four small accents.
  • TONEConfident and polished, with playful details—not a copy of existing branding.

04 / POSTER CONCEPT

Four voices.
One signal.

That’s the working idea for the final poster. Four beams of pink light meet at a single point: a simple visual metaphor for individuality coming together as a group.

A student concept and design rationale—not a BLACKPINK slogan, official artwork or a quote from the group.

FROM LOOKING TO DESIGNING

Observation → decision → reason.

These are observations of the official YG artist-profile page and my own design responses—not claims about an official brand rulebook.

YG PROFILE OBSERVATION [1] MY COMPLETED DESIGN RESPONSE MY RATIONALE
Large group-name treatment.
On YG’s profile, the group name is prominent [1]. The final poster places FOUR / voices. / ONE SIGNAL. at the top of its visual hierarchy. A clear first read helps someone understand the design before they notice the smaller details.
Group portrait.
The official profile presents the group together [1]. The poster’s four converging rays are an abstract, student-created symbol—not a portrait or official mark. A shared focal point suggests unity while keeping the concept original; it doesn’t pretend to show the members.
Open space around content.
This is my observation of the profile page [1]. The finished poster keeps one headline, one main image and a compact creator credit. Removing competing elements makes the hierarchy easier to follow and the message more legible.
A small coral accent.
This describes the page I observed, not an official palette rule [1]. Hot pink is my own accent for the rays and focal details. Limited colour contrast makes the focal point stand out. Pink is my design interpretation, not a verified official palette rule.
Separate artist-site sections.
Profile, discography and concert information appear in separate areas [1][2]. This portfolio also separates its research, timeline, design studies and source list. Consistent structure helps a viewer find information and helps me credit research clearly.

DESIGN LANGUAGE / APPLIED TO THE FINISHED POSTER

Choices with
a reason.

These terms explain the student-designed poster. They describe my decisions, not official BLACKPINK rules.

01 / HIERARCHY

What reads first?

The headline is the largest element, so the poster’s central phrase is noticed before the smaller credit.

02 / CONTRAST

What stands apart?

Hot pink is reserved for selected words and light accents against the dark background.

03 / NEGATIVE SPACE

Where can the eye rest?

Open dark areas separate the headline and light beams instead of filling every part of the page.

04 / REPETITION

What holds the series together?

The four-beam symbol and ink–pink colour relationship return across the poster and three-tile study.

05 / TYPOGRAPHY

How does the type speak?

A bold sans-serif gives the main phrase weight; the contrasting serif treatment makes “voices” feel more expressive.

06 / VISUAL METAPHOR

What does the image suggest?

Four separate lines meeting at one point are my abstract metaphor for distinct voices sharing one signal.

THE FINAL POSTER / COMPLETED DESIGN SPECIFICATION

One clear idea.
Built on purpose.

These are the finished poster’s actual design choices and the reasons behind them. The work is original and is not copied from an album or music video.

  1. HEADLINE / 01Four voices. One signal.

    The student-written phrase is the largest text. “Voices” uses a contrasting serif style to create a clear focal word.

  2. IMAGE / 02Four beams meet once.

    The original light-beam symbol sits behind the headline and converges at one bright point, giving the layout a single visual centre.

  3. PALETTE / 03Ink, pink, glow and paper.

    Near-black #211E23, hot pink #F72585, soft pink #F0C4E3 and warm white #F5F2EB are student-selected colours, not an official palette.

  4. TYPE / 04Contrast with a readability check.

    A bold sans-serif headline is paired with a smaller, plain supporting line. The final composition keeps its creator credit visible.

  5. CREDIT / 05Authorship is clear.

    The poster identifies itself as an original student design inspired by BLACKPINK; it does not claim endorsement or official status.
